How Korean Names Work
01
Family Name First
Korean names are written with the family name (성) first, followed by the given name. For example: Kim Taehyung (김태형) — Kim is the family name.
02
2 Syllable Given Names
Most Korean given names have 2 syllables. Each syllable (한자) carries a specific meaning, so Korean names are chosen for their meaning, not just sound.
03
Most Common Surnames
About 45% of Koreans have one of three surnames: Kim (김), Lee (이), or Park (박). Together they represent nearly half the population!
04
Meaning Matters
Korean parents carefully choose name characters for their meanings — like 지 (wisdom), 수 (excellence), 민 (cleverness), 현 (virtue).
Frequently Asked Questions
Can foreigners have a Korean name?
Yes! Many foreigners living in Korea, studying Korean, or fans of K-pop and K-dramas choose a Korean name. It is commonly used in workplaces, language schools, or simply for fun. Having a Korean name makes it easier for native speakers to address you and shows respect for the culture.
What is the most popular Korean name right now?
As of recent years, 민준 (Min-jun) is the most popular name for boys, and 서연 (Seo-yeon) tops the list for girls. These names are chosen for their beautiful Hanja meanings — Min-jun means "clever and talented," while Seo-yeon means "auspicious and beautiful."
Do Korean names have meanings?
Absolutely. Most Korean names are composed of Chinese characters called Hanja (한자), each carrying a specific meaning. For example, 민 (敏) means "clever," 준 (俊) means "talented," and 지 (智) means "wisdom." Parents spend a great deal of thought choosing characters that express their hopes for their child.
How do Korean surnames work?
Korean surnames (성, seong) come before the given name. There are only about 280 surnames in Korea, and just three — Kim (김), Lee (이), and Park (박) — account for nearly 45% of the entire population. Other common surnames include Choi (최), Jung (정), Kang (강), and Yoon (윤).
What is the difference between a Korean name and a K-pop stage name?
A Korean name is a person's official name registered at birth. A K-pop stage name is a separate artistic identity chosen by the artist or their agency — it may sound cool or be easy for international fans to remember. For example, BTS member V's real name is Kim Taehyung (김태형), while "V" is his stage name.
How do you pronounce Korean names in English?
Korean uses the Revised Romanization system to write Korean sounds in English letters. Each syllable is pronounced clearly and with roughly equal stress. For example, 지민 is Ji-min (jee-min), and 태형 is Tae-hyung (teh-hyung). The key is to pronounce each syllable separately rather than blending them together as in English.
Can I legally use a Korean name in Korea?
Foreigners cannot legally change their official name to Korean without citizenship, but you can freely use a Korean name socially, in school, or at work. Many expats and language learners adopt a Korean name for daily use. It is a common and welcomed practice in Korean culture.
Are Korean names unisex?
Some Korean names are unisex, such as 지민 (Ji-min) and 유진 (Yu-jin), which are used by both men and women. However, many names lean toward one gender based on the Hanja characters used. Names with characters meaning "strength" or "heroism" tend to be masculine, while names with characters meaning "grace" or "elegance" are often feminine.
Why do so many Koreans share the same surname?
Korean surnames have clan origins (본관, bon-gwan) tracing back hundreds of years. During the Joseon Dynasty, surname adoption became widespread and many families adopted the same common surnames. Today, there are over 600 Kim clans alone, all with different regional origins — so two people named Kim are not necessarily related.
